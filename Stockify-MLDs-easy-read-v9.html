Understanding MLDs

What is an MLD?

What is an MLD?

A Market Linked Debenture is a structured product. It pairs a debt component, which protects the principal, with exposure to an underlying such as the Nifty 50 or gold, which drives the return.

How are returns determined?

Each MLD sets out its payout before you invest: the underlying, the participation rate and the return range. Your return falls within that range, based on how the underlying performs.

Example: 1.7x participation in Nifty 50 gains, up to a maximum return of 51%.

How is the principal protected?

The principal is returned at maturity, subject to the issuer’s ability to pay. Some MLDs protect it fully. Others protect it against a set fall in the index, for example up to 20%.

Liquidity and taxation

MLDs are listed on BSE and can be sold before maturity, subject to demand. Gains are taxed at your income-tax slab rate.

Common MLD structures

Each structure defines its payout differently.

Fixed payout

A set return if the index stays above a defined level at maturity.

Example: 22.5% if the Nifty doesn’t fall over 20%.

Participation

A multiple of the index’s rise, up to a defined maximum.

Example: 1.7x of the Nifty 50’s rise, up to 51%.

G-Sec linked

Linked to a government bond, with a payout that is close to fixed.

Example: about 9.1% a year, 18 months.

The 5 key risksRead these before you apply
  • The company can default

    If it can’t pay, you may lose part or all of your money.

  • The condition can fail

    Then you get only your money back, after years with no return.

  • Gains are capped

    Even if the Nifty doubles, most MLDs pay a set maximum.

  • Hard to sell early

    Few buyers on BSE. Buy-backs are quarterly, at a small discount.

  • Taxed at your slab

    No long-term tax benefit, however long you hold.

Words you’ll see in a term sheetBarrier, cap, participation and 5 more
Start level

The Nifty level on the start date. Everything is measured from it.

Final level

The Nifty level at the end, often an average of a few days.

Barrier

The line the Nifty must stay above for you to earn the return.

Participation

How much of the Nifty’s rise you get. 120% means a 10% rise gives you 12%.

Cap

The most you can earn, however far the Nifty rises.

PP-MLD rating

Rates the company’s ability to pay you back. Not the market return.

Weekly value

An independent valuer says what your MLD is worth, every Friday.

Buy-back

Set dates when the company buys MLDs back early, at a small discount.

Yes, by selling on BSE or in the company’s quarterly buy-back. You may get a lower price.
