What is an MLD?

An MLD is a type of bond. You lend money to a trusted finance company for a few years.

  • 1
    You invest
    From ₹1 lakh, for about 1½ to 5 years.
  • 2
    Your return follows the market
    It depends on how an index like the Nifty 50 does.
  • 3
    You get paid at the end
    Your money back, plus any return you earned.
Returns are not fixed or guaranteed. If the market does badly, you may get only your money back.

Common questions

How does it work?

You invest once. The company uses most of your money to earn steady interest, and a small part to follow the market. At the end, you get your money back plus any market-linked return.

Is my principal protected?

Yes, at maturity, as long as the company can pay. That is why we only list MLDs from companies with strong credit ratings.

Will I get returns?

Not always. Your return depends on the market. If the market does well, you earn more. If it does badly, you may get only your money back.

Can I take my money out early?

It is possible but not easy. MLDs are best for money you won’t need until maturity.

Who can invest?

Resident Indians with PAN, KYC and a demat account.

How MLDs work, in plain English

You lend money to a finance company. You get it back at the end. On top, you may earn extra, depending on the Nifty.

Download the full guide (PDF)

3 simple steps

1

You invest

From ₹1 lakh, in an MLD from a rated finance company.

2

You wait

Usually 1½ to 5 years. Selling early is hard.

3

You get paid

Your money back, plus extra if the Nifty condition is met.

“Principal protected” is not risk-free. Your money comes back only if the company can pay. Always check its rating.

3 kinds of MLD

Each pays in a different way.

Fixed payout

A set amount if the Nifty stays above a line. Below it, just your money back.

Example: 22.5% if the Nifty doesn’t fall over 20%.

Share of the rise

You get part of the Nifty’s rise, often up to a limit. If it falls, your money back.

Example: 120% of the rise, up to 45%.

Close to fixed

Linked to a government bond, with a very easy condition. Works like an FD.

Example: about 9.1% a year, 18 months.

MLDs can suit you if

  • ✓You won’t need the money for 1½ to 5 years
  • ✓You can accept 0% in a bad market
  • ✓You choose highly rated companies

Skip MLDs if

  • ×You may need the money soon
  • ×You can’t afford years with no return
  • ×You want a fixed, known return

The 5 key risksRead these before you apply
  • The company can default

    If it can’t pay, you may lose part or all of your money.

  • The condition can fail

    Then you get only your money back, after years with no return.

  • Gains are capped

    Even if the Nifty doubles, most MLDs pay a set maximum.

  • Hard to sell early

    Few buyers on BSE. Buy-backs are quarterly, at a small discount.

  • Taxed at your slab

    No long-term tax benefit, however long you hold.

Words you’ll see in a term sheetBarrier, cap, participation and 5 more
Start level

The Nifty level on the start date. Everything is measured from it.

Final level

The Nifty level at the end, often an average of a few days.

Barrier

The line the Nifty must stay above for you to earn the return.

Participation

How much of the Nifty’s rise you get. 120% means a 10% rise gives you 12%.

Cap

The most you can earn, however far the Nifty rises.

PP-MLD rating

Rates the company’s ability to pay you back. Not the market return.

Weekly value

An independent valuer says what your MLD is worth, every Friday.

Buy-back

Set dates when the company buys MLDs back early, at a small discount.
